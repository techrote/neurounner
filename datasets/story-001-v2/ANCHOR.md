# STORY-001 corrective single-story anchor

This corrective pass supersedes the stitched eight-variant v1 story as the accepted narrative target while preserving v1 as evidence.

## User requirements

- Produce **one continuous short story**, not eight variants or eight quasi-independent sections.
- The **single story itself must be at least 3,000 words**.
- Synthesize the strongest material from all eight v1 variants rather than concatenating them.
- Reduce reliance on the standalone first-person pronoun `I`; remain first-person, but prefer sensory/object-led syntax, implied subjects, `my`/`me`, participial constructions, and sentences led by machinery, environment, or bodily sensation.
- Preserve Sprocket-like uncertainty and tactile industrial detail.
- Do not present the fictional incident as historical canon.

## Primary style/incident anchor

The following passage is the user's favourite and should strongly influence the central rescue sequence, without forcing a verbatim copy or repeated reset:

> The smell hit me first, thick and wrong, like burnt copper and wet wool. It wasn't the clean ozone of a standard fault; this was the scent of something organic being ground into the metal. My boots clicked on the slick grating as the belt roared closer, the heat radiating against my skin, the line stopping just inches from my grasp. The companion stood on the wrong side of the moving metal, their face twisted in a silent scream that I could not hear. I had to act fast, but my movements felt slow, heavy with the weight of a failing system. I lunged forward, grabbing the edge of the moving chain, but the friction burned my palms raw. I yanked hard, trying to pull them back, but the metal held firm, unyielding. The rescue failed instantly. There was no time to hesitate, no room for a second attempt. The belt surged forward, swallowing the gap between us, and I was left holding only the cold, slick edge of the machine. I tried to stop the machine, to break the loop, but the logic was too strong. The gears spun on, indifferent to the life trapped inside the casing. I stood there, trapped in the wrong side of the machinery, waiting for the line stop. The smell remained, the only proof that something had gone terribly wrong.

## Narrative qualities to retain

- burnt copper / wet wool / hot insulation / oily steel;
- slick grating, moving belt/chain, heat and friction burns;
- a companion on the wrong side of the machinery;
- a rescue attempt that fails immediately and physically;
- machinery that is indifferent rather than anthropomorphically evil;
- a salvage/signal intervention that is only partly understood;
- a brownout and route from the childhood city-zone into the autofactory district;
- faulty memory expressed through one or two precise contradictions, not wholesale continuity collapse;
- the missing/whistling noise as a memory residue;
- causality that remains physically legible from entry through failure, intervention, line stop and aftermath.

## Explicit anti-patterns

- no `---`-separated variants;
- no repeated re-introductions of the incident;
- no repeated opening paragraph or repeated "the smell hit me first" resets;
- no generic heroic resolve loops;
- no invented reactor-core / destiny / hologram / data-stream lore;
- no explaining the machine's internal logic as if Sprocket has omniscient engineering knowledge;
- no meta-commentary about prompts, variants, storyboards, evidence files, or being an AI.
